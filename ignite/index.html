7-Day Ignite Starter

One week, about 30 minutes a day, no gym required. This isn't a crash diet or a punishment workout. It's how you start again and actually keep going.

Why I made this
I used to be 100 pounds heavier. I know what day one feels like.

I remember not knowing where to start, eating 2,000-calorie fast food meals without thinking twice, and feeling out of place the second I walked into a gym. What changed things wasn't motivation. It was small wins, stacked every day, until I trusted myself again.

That's what this week is. Seven days of small wins. Tick each day off as you go.

— Ryan, Blaize Fitness

The rules

  1. Done beats perfect. A short workout still counts. A missed day isn't a failed week.
  2. Go at your level. Every move has an easier option. Use it with zero shame.
  3. Stop if something hurts, as in sharp pain, not just effort. If you have an injury or health condition, check with your doctor before starting anything new.
  4. Message me. Stuck on a move or a meal? DM me on Instagram. I read them myself.
Day 1

Know your why

Before you change anything, get clear on why it matters. Write one sentence: "I'm doing this because…" Make it personal. Keeping up with your kids beats "losing weight."

Your baseline

  • Take a front and side photo private
  • Walk 10 minutes, then note how you feel 1–10
  • Count your push-ups (wall or knees is fine) max reps

You'll redo this on Day 7. Most people are surprised.

Day 2

Workout A

Do 3 rounds. Rest 60–90 seconds between rounds. Move with control. Speed comes later.

Workout A · ~25 min

  • Squat to a chair 10 reps
  • Incline push-up (counter or wall) 8 reps
  • Glute bridge 12 reps
  • Plank (knees okay) 20 sec
Day 3

Fix one meal

You don't need a perfect diet. Pick the one meal that goes off the rails most often and make it better. Same restaurant, smarter order.

The swap formula

  • Build it around protein first chicken, eggs, fish, beans
  • Add a fruit or veggie 1 fist
  • Swap the soda or sweet tea water or zero-cal
  • Keep the carbs, go smaller half portion

My old Chipotle order was a burrito plus 3 tacos, about 1,900 calories. Now it's a double-chicken bowl, around 720. I still leave full.

Day 4

Workout B

Do 3 rounds. Rest 60–90 seconds between rounds.

Workout B · ~25 min

  • Split squat (hold a wall) 8 each leg
  • Backpack row (bent over) 10 reps
  • Dead bug 8 each side
  • Step-ups on a stair 10 each leg
Day 5

Get your heart rate up

Steps are great for health, but they don't always push your heart hard enough. Today, do 20 minutes where you can talk but couldn't sing.

Pick one · 20 min

  • Brisk walk up a hill West Seattle has plenty
  • Bike or elliptical steady pace
  • Walk 2 min, faster 1 min repeat
Day 6

Workout A, level up

Same workout as Day 2, with one change: add a 4th round, or add 2 reps to every move. That's progress, and it's how every strong person started.

Day 7

Look back, then go forward

Redo your Day 1 baseline: the 10-minute walk and your push-up count. Then answer two questions honestly: What felt easier than you expected? And what got in your way?

That second answer is the most useful thing you learned this week. It's exactly what a real plan should be built around.

You started. Now keep going.
